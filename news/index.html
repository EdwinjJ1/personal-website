🔁 AI4AI-Bench — Benchmarking LLM Agents in Algorithmic Design for Recursive Self-Improvement
Yizhe Chi et al. isolate a single scientific question: can an LLM agent design a better training algorithm? 10 frozen research repositories span 10 training-algorithm families. In each task an agent has 4 hours on one B300 to rewrite the training algorithm; the code is rerun from scratch for up to 12 hours and scored by a hidden evaluator against the repo's original algorithm under matched compute. No agent can win by collecting more data or tuning hyperparameters — only by changing the algorithm itself. The cleanest test yet of recursive self-improvement; watch whether frontier labs take the leaderboard seriously. arXiv: 2608.20318.
→ Paper
🎯 Phantom Gains — Auditing Self-Improvement Against a Measured Null
Cheng Xu et al. push an untrained frozen control through the same LoRA self-training pipeline used in their three self-improvement rounds on Qwen3-8B and surface seven measurement artifacts, each of which inverts a reported finding without the control. A greedy-decode ledger manufactures capability changes on the untrained model (mostly an inference-batching artifact); the popular "expansion statistic" assigns that same model a 0.280 acquisition rate; the natural threshold repair does not survive replication. They replace it with a per-problem exact test against a pooled baseline. Required reading for anyone publishing self-improvement numbers — reviewer rebuttals will start citing this within weeks. arXiv: 2608.20290.
→ Paper
🔧 MidTool — Mid-training Data Synthesis for Agentic Tool Use
Fengqing Jiang, Yuxiong He et al. argue mid-training (not just SFT or RL) is the right stage to bake agentic tool use into a base model. MidTool is an open corpus-construction pipeline combining large-scale web/PDF/code data with synthesized supervision from real tool APIs, MCP skills, and document-grounded workflows. Targets four behaviours: recognize tool affordances, ground arguments, compose call workflows, recover from incomplete info. Mid-training Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base on MidTool-Mix then continuing with SFT+RL beats baselines on agentic tool-use benchmarks. Open pipeline; recipe is the contribution. arXiv: 2608.20314.
→ Paper
📦 Break It Down, Pass It On — Cross-Task Skill Transfer in LLM Agents
Yiyang Feng et al. run a controlled study of how skills are induced from completed tasks and how well they transfer. Two axes: task-level vs. subtask-level induction, and text vs. code skill formats. Result: task-level skills mostly push the agent below its no-memory baseline; subtask-level skills lift it above on average; text skills transfer better than code skills. Specificity (match to real tasks) and abstractness (relevance distribution) explain most of the variance. Concrete recipe for memory-bank agents: store subtask-level, text-format skills, not monolithic task summaries. arXiv: 2608.20274.
→ Paper
⚙️ Learning When to Think — Adaptive Reasoning for Test-Time Compute Allocation
Gijs Kassenaar et al. let the model choose its own reasoning depth inside GRPO. The model emits a first-token mode flag — NoThink (answer fast), Short, or Long — learned end-to-end via a shaped reward plus per-mode token caps so modes don't collapse. On a 1.5B distilled model trained on MATH, the three modes emerge cleanly and Short beats Long in accuracy — evidence the router sorts problems by difficulty rather than just running longer. Cleanest small-model proof yet that self-gated test-time compute is learnable with GRPO alone. arXiv: 2608.20256.

🧠 StagedWorkspace — A Versioned Workspace for Knowledge-Work Agents
Anonymous (under review) proposes a workspace-state contract: every parsed view, review diff, and submitted artifact must be explicitly tied to a content hash of the evolving native file. StagedWorkspace binds parsed records and review diffs to those hashes. With dual parsed/native access, Gemini 3.1 Pro jumps from 29.3% to 63.9% on OfficeQA Pass@1 (+34.6), and GPT-5.4 Nano climbs from 25.5 to 42.1 on APEX mean rubric score. Single-view baselines lose 8.3–12.1 points on OfficeQA and 4.7–9.2 on APEX. Most "agent failures" are actually version-confusion bugs; treating workspace state as a first-class contract looks like one of the cheapest, most general SOTA wins we've seen — drops into coding, docs, spreadsheet, slide, and notebook agents. arXiv: 2608.18050.
→ Paper
🔤 TokEval — A Tokenizer Evaluation Suite (COLM 2026)
Clara Meister et al. ship a principled tokenizer evaluation framework that goes beyond fertility and compression rate to capture linguistically and structurally meaningful properties — UTF-8 character boundary integrity, digit place-value alignment, etc. Controlled pretraining experiments varying only the tokenizer's training mixture / pretokenization / algorithm show that information-theoretic metrics predict language modeling ability (Spearman ρ up to 0.80), while structure-sensitive metrics correlate with downstream task accuracy across linguistic, math, and code benchmarks. Tokenizers are picked with almost no evaluation today, yet they shape every downstream capability. TokEval lets teams replace expensive pretraining sweeps with intrinsic measurement before committing millions of FLOPs. arXiv: 2608.18062.
→ Paper
🎨 Tuned Diffusion Sampling with Bayesian Optimization (OYS)
Travis Zhang et al. treat timestep selection in diffusion sampling as a black-box optimization problem and optimize sample quality directly with Bayesian Optimization. OYS beats both default schedules and "Align Your Steps" on text-to-image generation and improves over defaults on inpainting and other image tasks, in both quantitative and human evaluations. No additional training; works on distilled models; works with simple Euler and DPM-Solver++. A 5-step OYS schedule retains 89–94% of a 50-step schedule's quality while reducing inference cost by 10×. Drop-in scheduler upgrade for any diffusion pipeline — the kind of result that ships to prod next quarter. arXiv: 2608.18040.

🔬 AutoResearch — Insight In, Hallucination Out
Anonymous authors present a two-stage autonomous research system: Idea Generation continuously fuses emerging research signals with accumulated domain knowledge and uses multi-model generation + cross-review to produce grounded, testable research plans; Idea Execution decomposes those plans into experiments with coordinated agents and an independent evidence-based review before accepting conclusions. Across cross-modal retrieval, systems optimization, and benchmark-driven ML, AutoResearch turns generated ideas into measurable progress, detects and corrects unreliable experimental results, and makes evidence-conditioned continue/revise/terminate decisions. On RSICD, an AutoResearch-generated idea lifts mean Recall from 32.84 to 34.69 with only 5 audit-confirmed issue events vs. 11–27 for competing systems — a 2–5× reduction in hallucinated results. arXiv: 2608.17906.

🎮 Procedural Content Metageneration via Program Search and Continual Abstraction Discovery
Julian Togelius et al. (IEEE CoG 2026) evolve complete Python procedural content generators for Sokoban, Zelda, Dangerous Dave, and Lode Runner using language-model mutation and crossover. The key contribution is CAD (Continual Abstraction Discovery), which extracts reusable primitives from high-fitness programs into a run-specific helper module. A 2×2 experiment crosses CAD with access to a fixed hand-written domain API across 160 complete runs. CAD raises mean final best fitness in all 8 domain×API comparisons; learned libraries are adopted by most later programs and repeatedly rediscover validation, reachability, and structural utilities. Strong signal that LLM-driven evolution + library discovery is a viable path for open-ended content and tool synthesis. arXiv: 2608.17947.
